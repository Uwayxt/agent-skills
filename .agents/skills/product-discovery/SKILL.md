# Product Discovery

**Product discovery surfaces the bedrock truth of user pain before writing a single line of code.** Everything built on false premises rots.

### 1. Frame the problem
State the core problem without proposing a solution. What is the fundamental struggle?
Use structured interview questions:
- "Walk me through the last time you tried to do X."
- "What was the hardest part about that process?"
- "Why was that hard?"

### 2. Map the actors
Identify every stakeholder who touches the system. Use this persona table template format:
| Persona | Goal | Current Pain | Frequency of Action |

### 3. Identify existing solutions & workarounds
Document what the user does TODAY without your product. Note the spreadsheets, the duct tape, the manual syncing. These workarounds are the raw material for your product's value proposition.

### 4. Scan the landscape
Map out direct and indirect competitors. Analyze their positioning, strengths, and weaknesses. Identify where they fail to solve the core problem.

### 5. Document assumptions
List the riskiest assumptions that must be true for this product to succeed. Prioritize them by risk and uncertainty.

### Completion criteria
- Problem is stated in user-centric terms, not feature-centric terms.
- Actor map is complete with specific pain points.
- Existing workarounds are documented.
- Riskiest assumptions are explicitly listed.

### Output format
Produce a single `discovery_synthesis.md` artifact containing the problem frame, actor map, workarounds, and assumptions.

### Next Steps
Once discovery is validated, proceed to `product-strategy` for prioritization and `business-model-thinking` for monetization analysis.

### Anti-patterns
- Skipping discovery because the founder "already knows" the problem.
- Confusing a feature request with a validated problem.
- Discovery that produces a 20-page report nobody reads — keep it to one artifact.
